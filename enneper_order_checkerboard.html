Multi-saddle checkerboard

Geometry & order

The slider selects the exponent m in the generalized Enneper family, g(w)=wᵐ. Order 1 is the classical Enneper surface. Increasing m produces a higher-order saddle with m+1 rising and m+1 falling height sectors.

For w=u+iv:
x=Re[w−w²ᵐ⁺¹/(2m+1)]
y=−Im[w+w²ᵐ⁺¹/(2m+1)]
z=2 Re[wᵐ⁺¹]/(m+1).

This is one connected surface, not multiple separate saddles. Checkerboard cells follow its parameter grid; their edges are generally curved. Large patches can self-intersect.

Drag to rotate · Scroll to zoom · Increase the order for more saddle sectors

Order & appearance

Try orders 1, 2, 3 and 5 with radius near 1. Increasing radius reveals more folding; at high orders, large radii amplify the outer lobes dramatically. Black and white alternate across parameter-cell edges, including on the back of the surface.
